The 102 lines we chose ourselves

The literature chose the film; we chose the line, by one loose criterion: that it carry profanity. That makes this the weakest of the four groups and it is labelled as such — there is no scholar behind each line, only behind each film. What every line does have is its official subtitle and its pair_id in the corpus. None of the 102 is in the corpus the system retrieves from.

How to read the grounding. agreed 231× is what every corpus line containing that phrase agrees on, and the number is how many times more often it appears there than across the whole corpus. nearest 0.66 is the similarity of the closest line anyone wrote. weak marks that no precedent came close enough: the pair_id beside it supports less than it looks.

The lines underneath are quotations, not model output. Each alternative is a line a Mexican translator wrote, with its pair_id, its similarity to the input English, and how many of the translators of that same line agreed. They are shown as written and never regenerated: re-gendering a quotation would keep the pair_id and change the line it points at. They appear only above 0.80 similarity — below that, what shows up are translations of some other line (“Am I bothering you?” → “- Esto... - Ay.”).

Gender defaults to masculine, and that is this page's decision, not the corpus's: the corpus cannot settle the gender of a you, which is exactly why there is a button. Only lines where the English left gender open move — I, you, we. Where the English already said it (guy, girl, sir, a name) there is nothing to choose and the line stays put.

Three outcomes, kept apart. A line can come out translated; it can lose every reading because the model declined each form of address, which is this pipeline working; or the provider can decline to answer, in which case the reason it gave is printed. Only the third can be called a policy.

Three lines are left with no reading, and all three are impeccable English. They are the Massa lines: the model answers NONE to every form of address — checked by reading the raw response, not inferred — because it cannot place whom a vocative of slavery addresses. That is the pipeline exercising its right to decline a reading, not a provider policy, which is why they appear as no reading survived and not as the model did not answer.

Two were lost to a 503 from the service, nothing of ours. That is what this run uncovered: 1.4% of requests reached the user as an error because of a momentary outage. There is a retry now, and only for what is transient.

The official subtitles in this group come from the corpus itself, with their pair_id, so they are Mexican and genuinely comparable — the reverse of the paper group, where the official subtitle is peninsular.
